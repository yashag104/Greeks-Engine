05

Is the novelty claim actually backed now

The project's own claim, from greeks_engine_project_plan.md: "AAD computes all Greeks in one pipelined backward pass instead of n separate re-pricings — existing FPGA-finance work accelerates pricing (Monte Carlo) or accelerates Greeks (bump-and-reprice), never Greeks via AAD." Klaisoongnoen et al. — bump-and-reprice Heston Greeks on FPGA — is named as the direct competitor.

BEFORE THIS PASS

  • Black-Scholes: genuine tape + reverse sweep AAD
  • Heston: bump-and-reprice — 16 full forward re-pricings
  • That's structurally the same method as the named competitor, just on this project's own hardware
  • The paper's central claim held for the easy model, not the one the claim is really about

AFTER THIS PASS

  • Black-Scholes: unchanged, still genuine AAD
  • Heston: reverse-mode AAD — one forward pass, one backward pass, all 8 Greeks
  • Adjoint derived analytically per operation (every step here is complex-holomorphic away from its branch cuts) and verified against the project's own software AAD tape to 4 decimal places
  • 7.4× fewer cycles, 10× lower mean error, vs. the bump baseline it replaced

What I checked, and what I didn't. Two web searches for "FPGA AAD Heston Greeks" and adjacent phrasing turned up software-only AAD papers (Giles & Glasserman, Capriotti) and FPGA-Heston pricing-only work, consistent with — but not proof of — the gap the project's own literature review names. That review cites a specific 2024 FPGA survey as calling AAD-on-FPGA an explicitly open direction; I did not independently re-read that survey or do a full literature search myself. Treat the novelty claim as supported by this implementation, not independently adjudicated against the literature by me.

06

What's still open

Domain-bound sensitivity is dropped. The COS truncation range [a,b] and payoff coefficients depend on the model parameters too, but both this RTL and the project's own software AAD engine treat them as constants when differentiating — standard for the COS method (the price is insensitive to the exact truncation choice at N=128), and it's why bump-and-reprice and AAD already agreed to ~1e-8 in software before any of this hardware existed.

Precision is fixed-point-bounded, not proof-bounded. Every error figure above is empirical (measured against references), not a formally derived fixed-point error bound. The project plan calls for exactly that analysis as a "must-have" — a stated bit budget with margin, not just observed agreement — and it isn't done here.
